Roadmap

What coraine has now, and what is planned - short, medium and long term, one line each. coraine is an Incubated FIWARE Generic Enabler; the overall roadmap of FIWARE is in the section "Roadmap" of the FIWARE Catalogue.

This roadmap elaborates on proposed new features or tasks which are expected to be added to the product in the foreseeable future. There should be no assumption of a commitment to deliver these features on specific dates or in the order given. The development team will be doing their best to follow the proposed dates and priorities, but please bear in mind that plans to work on a given feature or task may be revised. All information is provided as general guidelines only, and this page may be revised to provide newer information at any time.

The roadmap is kept current as work lands, not at release boundaries. Every idea, with what there is to say about it, is in Ideas - an item's more links to it; the day-to-day backlog, with what is deferred by design and why, is the backlog in detail.

Available now

Recently built, and documented where it lives:

  • An NGSI-LD plugin for APISIX - ngsild-pep (corApisixNgsild): fdsc.so's own decision in the gateway the FIWARE Data Space Connector already uses, in front of any NGSI-LD broker; on the 64 cases of test/fdscDiff it decides as --auth fdsc does. --fdscNgsild stays in the broker: it needs the stored entity (Authorization - Inside a Data Space Connector).
  • DDS - topics both ways, services, actions, through the DDS bridge (DDS and ROS 2).
  • MQTT - device topics as Channels, and notifications to mqtt:// (The MQTT bridge).
  • Modbus TCP - registers as Channels (The Modbus bridge).
  • cor:// - the binary protocol for GE-to-GE traffic: forwarding without a JSON parse, many requests in flight on one connection (The cor format and cor://).
  • Requests that wait run as coroutines of the event loops, not on worker threads (Coroutines).
  • corDB on disk - --dbDir: an append log synced every 100 ms and snapshots; the store survives a restart, still with no database server (Installation - corDB on disk).
  • Temporal history in corDB - --troe corDB: the whole temporal API on the history corDB keeps itself, on disk with the store - no PostgreSQL, no second plugin.
  • Service Execution - "do this" in the API, not a write to an attribute: Service Descriptions, executions, synchronous and asynchronous, combined (GR CIM-055). scopeQ - a subscription's scope query, on the target entity's scope - selects the entities of a grouped execution (alone or with the other selectors) and narrows the entities a Service Registration offers its service on. A grouped or combined execution, and a Combined Service Template (created or PATCHed), refuses a member it does not know, at every level (400, naming it). A service name is unique within an entity: a registration (created or PATCHed) whose serviceName another registration of the tenant has, for entities both could select, is refused - 409 naming that registration. Two selectors are taken as disjoint only when that is certain: different types, different ids, an id the other's idPattern does not match, two anchored idPatterns with literal prefixes neither of which starts the other; q, geoQ and scopeQ do not separate. An entity that still has a name twice (an entity of two types, each with a registration of the name) answers an invocation of it with 409 naming the registrations.
  • Snapshots, on corDB too - § 5.16 complete: writes and subscriptions on a Snapshot, on both stores.
  • The WebSocket transport - subscriptions and notifications over a WebSocket (WebSocket).
  • Server-Sent Events - a subscription on urn:ngsi-ld:self made on its own event stream: the 201 to its POST is text/event-stream, its notifications the events, and it lives as long as the stream (Server-Sent Events). Over a WebSocket, urn:ngsi-ld:self is the connection.
  • Array reduction of entity data - one-element arrays reduced at the input, by the @context.
  • make tune - the broker built for your workload: the profile trained on it, the allocator and the lock policy measured on it (Extreme performance).
  • Subscriptions at scale - a write matched only against the subscriptions that can match it: +32 % with 1 000 subscriptions (Performance).
  • An NGSI-LD-aware JSON parser - the core terms are an enum, not strings: a compare instead of a strcmp wherever a member is recognised.
  • linux/arm64 - every image for amd64 and arm64 under one tag (arm64 without the DDS bridge), and the whole functional suite on native ARM runners every night.
  • Migrating from Orion-LD - coraine-import, a program of its own on the broker's libraries: entities, subscriptions, registrations and the temporal history, with their ids and system timestamps (Migrating a database to coraine). Later, the ETSI neutral export format.
  • Debian packages - apt install coraine (and coraine-dev: the exact source and coraine-build) for Ubuntu 26.04, 24.04 and Debian 13, amd64 and arm64, from the signed repository https://seamware.github.io/apt (Installation - Install with apt).
  • Running on Kubernetes - a memory budget that refuses work instead of being killed for it, a health port answering /live and /ready outside the HTTP queue, and a byte budget per query response - entity, temporal and distributed queries (Kubernetes).
  • Running outside a container - --daemon to detach, and the log in a file, on stdout or both (--logTo, --logDir); the broker stays in the foreground, logging to stdout, by default. -fg is accepted until 0.8.0 (Administration - Foreground or daemon).
  • Authorization in the broker - --auth: auth plugins, and fdsc.so, the FIWARE Data Space Connector's access tokens and ODRL policies decided inside the broker as the connector decides them - 64 cases against a deployed connector, 0 differences; per entity, per attribute and per tenant with --fdscNgsild, notifications decided for the subscription's owner; the ODRL-PAP's /policy API served by the broker (--policyApiPort) (Authorization).
  • The CKAN bridge and DataChannels - a CKAN portal's DataStore resources read into entities (ckan.so, corCkanBridge); a DataChannel makes each record (a row) into the entities its mapping names (DataChannels). A bridge reports a Channel's status, and why, at any time (channelStatusIn).
  • c³, the coraine Control Center - Compile - a broker built for a deployment from a web page: the features in or out, each with its size, the bridges, the build mode (release, PGO, make tune, debug), on coraine-dev's coraine-build (more).
  • corDB persistence, cheaper writes - a write that updates attributes logs those attributes, not the entity: PATCH +11-13 % on disk (Performance).
  • Declared indexes - an attribute queried often is declared, and the broker keeps an index of its values for q: on corDB and MongoDB (--dbValueIndexes) and on the TimescaleDB history (--troeValueIndexes), in every tenant, a tenant created later included; listed in /admin/indexes and /admin/metrics (Extreme performance).
  • A faster TimescaleDB history - TRoE writers and unnested rows (5-11x the history write rate), the history database down without the current state going down (held, retried, then counted as lost), and --troeSync for read-your-writes (Performance).
  • Timepoint (NGSI-LD v2.1) - an entity, or a query's entities, as they were at a point in time, from the history inside corDB (Timepoint).
  • The stored trees in less RAM - the tree heap and 32-byte scalar nodes: broker RSS -14.5 % at 100 000 entities; a plugin built for another node layout is refused at start, with a message (Performance - The stored trees).
  • The OPC UA bridge - Variables as attributes, Methods as Service Executions, the server's node tree as the bridge's inventory, SignAndEncrypt with certificates (--bridges opcua, corOpcuaBridge).
  • The FIWARE IoT Agents as bridge plugins - prototypes - JSON, UltraLight 2.0, LwM2M, LoRaWAN, Sigfox and ISOXML, each a public repository of its own (corIota*Bridge); OPC UA is the OPC UA bridge (FIWARE IoT Agents).
  • What a running broker is - GET /admin/config (every option it runs with, a secret masked), whether its build has traces (/admin/log), a bridge's inventory for a mapping tool (/admin/bridges/{bridge}/inventory) (Administration - The admin API).
  • c³ - Operate and Connect - brokers started and stopped, natively or in a container, with their services; their logs, log and trace levels, configuration and metrics as charts; the entity editor, and signing in to a broker's security (an OpenID provider, or the Data Space Connector's verifier) (more).
  • The documentation in c³, and as a website - the guides of c³'s Documentation page, written from coraine's documentation for the reader, searchable, offline in c³; the same pages at seamware.github.io/docs.
  • EntityMaps the broker creates itself - a distributed query, and a local query paginated past its first page, freezes its set of matching entities on the first page and serves the pages from it (the links carry the map's id); an entity that no longer matches is left out of its page (EntityMaps).

Bridges and Channels are coraine's own mechanism, not a standard. The concept goes to the ETSI TC DATA face-to-face in Athens, 20-22 October 2026, and anything normative will realistically follow in 2027. coraine implements its own objects now and adapts to whatever TC DATA settles on.

Short term

Planned for the short term and the next release of the product, in roughly this order:

  • c³ - Configure: the DDS mapping tool - DDS topics, services and actions to entities and attributes, in the Control Center: the mapping file the DDS bridge reads, made from the bridge's inventory (/admin/bridges/{bridge}/inventory) (more).
  • WebSocket subscriptions of a crashed instance, under High Availability - each instance's run id and a heartbeat in the shared store, so that any instance deletes the WebSocket subscriptions of a run whose heartbeat stopped - also when a killed pod never comes back. Without --high-availability a broker deletes them when it starts (more).
  • c³ Documentation, the rest - a guide per cor-lib with an API reference from its headers, the pages in coraine-dev for offline use, and a skeleton repository per kind of plugin (c3 new plugin).
  • Subordinate subscriptions on registration change - § 10.5.2.4 for PATCH too (more).

Medium term

Proposed for the medium term, typically within the subsequent releases of the next 9 months:

  • c³ - Configure: the OPC UA mapping tool - OPC UA variables, monitored items and methods to entities and attributes, as for DDS (more).
  • Live public transport: a GTFS-realtime bridge, and buses moving in c³ - a transit agency's routes (a LineString each), stops and trips from its static GTFS; its vehicles' live positions (GTFS-realtime, Protocol Buffers over HTTP, polled) as Bus entities related to their trip, route and next stop; c³ subscribes over its WebSocket and moves each bus on the map as the notifications arrive. First feed: Boston's MBTA, beside the Boston CKAN datasets (more).
  • corDB history: retention and a selector - history kept for a time or a size, and recording only what it is told to (entities, attributes - subscription-shaped); after ETSI has decided on it (more).
  • More bridges - Kafka, one standard binding across the brokers that have one, on the Bridge/Channel seam that carries DDS, MQTT, Modbus, OPC UA and CKAN (more).
  • The IoT Agents as cor-agent plugins, released - the six prototypes (JSON, UltraLight, LWM2M, LoRaWAN, Sigfox, ISOXML; OPC UA is the OPC UA bridge) brought to parity with the FIWARE IoT Agents and released; one binary or two tiers (more).
  • The design notes as guides - Service Execution, the Cor Data Space Connector, the IoT Agents as Service Executors and their measures as DataChannel records: today design notes in coraine's documentation, guides once their designs settle.
  • A CSV bridge, and CKAN beyond once - CSV files as DataChannels, as CKAN resources are; CKAN's poll (the dataset's activity stream) and push (a webhook) modes (more).
  • NGSIv2 support - the NGSIv2 API as an API plugin beside NGSI-LD, on the same stores: a way off Orion (NGSIv2) for deployments that cannot change their clients at once, and the other half of migrating from Orion together with the NGSIv2 reader for coraine-import (more).
  • Aligning Bridges and Channels with ETSI - 2027, after the Athens face-to-face (more).
  • haaux - high-availability cache sync without a shared database (more).
  • Scaling out: sharding by tenant, then by entity type - corDB stays linked into every broker; tenants routed by APISIX on NGSILD-Tenant, types by routing coraines over distributed operations - no load balancer of our own (more).
  • HA peer push - with a shared database, the broker that changes a subscription, registration or @context pushes the change to the other brokers itself (cor://) and answers the client once they have it - the window in which an update reaching another broker misses a notification shrinks from the change stream's ~50 ms to a LAN round trip. The cluster's members in a collection of the shared database (more).
  • Bridges and Channels over the API - create, update, delete, persisted (more).
  • One compound geo index - one index for every GeoProperty, both variants prototyped and measured.
  • Hot and cold entity segments - the entities written often apart from the rest (a design note first).
  • Our own @context server - the JSON-LD context hosting API (/jsonldContexts) as a small C server on our own libraries, native on every architecture; it replaces the Java wistefan/context-server (amd64 only) in the tests and CI, and can host the contexts a deployment publishes.
  • Removing what nobody reads - garbage collection of expired transient Entities, and of the entities a bridge's source no longer has; to be discussed in ETSI first (more).

Long term

Proposals for the longer-term evolution of the product. There is no commitment to deliver them in a specific timeframe; they are here so that potential contributors can see where the product is heading and may wish to get involved.

  • corDB standalone - corDB as a server of its own beside the brokers, for high availability (the haaux role), with backups of current state and history (more).
  • Our own string collation, replacing ICU - root collation without 39 MiB of libicu (more).
  • Array reduction for API objects - subscriptions, registrations, ... - once the core context has @set for their arrays (entity data is done) (more).
  • Embedded deployment - constrained hardware as a build configuration (more).
  • c³ - Connect - the Control Center connected live to a running broker over its WebSocket transport (queries, then a subscription's notifications): its entities, their relationships as a graph (boxes and arrows, zoom, pan, entities dragged into place), the GeoProperty entities on a map, their values as they change; also as an API plugin of the broker itself (more).
  • A smaller DDS stack for the DDS bridge - for where the bridge has to run: the DDS libraries are today the largest part of a coraine installation (about five times the broker), which is why the arm64 image and the Debian packages come without the DDS bridge, and a device agent on a small board needs far less. A DDS implementation in C, or the subset of the DDS wire protocol (RTPS) the bridge uses - discovery, reliable and best-effort readers and writers, CDR - on the same Bridge/Channel seam; interoperability with every DDS and ROS 2 node is unchanged, it is the wire protocol (more).
  • Cold storage for history in Parquet - older history moved out of the TRoE store into Parquet files (on disk or object storage), compressed and columnar, queried through embedded DuckDB: cheap storage for years of history and fast wide aggregations. Recent history, and its ingest, stay in the hot store. Not for current state.

  • Service Execution, following GR CIM-055 as it settles - the status names, the query and discovery parameters.
  • Continuous ETSI conformance - 100 % kept as the specification evolves (more).
